# Street lights and late foraging in city honeybees

*Sample paper written for this demo. The study, the numbers and the authors are invented.*

We asked whether street lights make city honeybees forage later in the evening. We watched 12 hives for 40 evenings: 6 hives within 50 m of a street light and 6 hives in dark parks. Each evening we counted the bees that came back to the hive after sunset. Bees near street lights kept returning for 38 minutes after sunset on average, while bees in dark parks stopped after 14 minutes. The late bees carried less pollen per trip, about two thirds of the load of daytime bees. Hives near lights did not grow faster over the summer, so the extra trips did not seem to pay off. We found no difference on cloudy evenings, when the light from the sky was already low. We conclude that street lights stretch the foraging day but not the food supply, and we suggest warmer, dimmer lights near parks and gardens.
